# Nano Banana Prompting Guide

This guide outlines strategies and guidelines for writing high-quality prompts to get the best out of the Nano Banana engines.

## What to think about while writing prompts
Use detailed prompts to take more control over the images you generate. Think about what you want to see – the characters, the setting, and the overall feel. The more detail you add, the closer the image will be to what you’ve imagined.

### 1. Style (or Medium)
What kind of image are you creating?
- **Examples**: Illustration, photograph, watercolor painting, 3D render, anime.
- **Aesthetic**: Retro-futuristic, abstract, cinematic, hyper-detailed.

### 2. Subject
Who or what is the main focus of the image?
- **Examples**: A snow leopard, a woman with magenta rim lighting, a robotic character.

### 3. Setting
Where is the scene taking place?
- **Examples**: Snowy mountain pass, dark textured studio, lush tropical jungle.

### 4. Action
What is the subject doing?
- **Examples**: Walking through a pass, staring into the camera, holding hands.

### 5. Composition
How is the camera positioned and the scene framed?
- **Examples**: Eye-level shot, profile portrait, close-up, dramatic rim lighting.

---

## Editing with prompts
After creating an image, you can keep editing it by tweaking specific details:
- **Change the character**: Swap your subject for someone or something else, or add extra characters, while keeping the same style, setting, and composition.
- **Adjust the composition**: Change camera angles, framing, or distance.
- **Alter the action**: Modify what the characters are doing.
- **Swap the setting**: Change the environment/background.
- **Rethink the style**: Convert a photo to an illustration or change the color grading.

## General prompting techniques

### 1. Visualize Real-World Knowledge
Describe how you'd like the model to organize information (e.g. asking for a timeline, flowchart, or craft-style diagram). Reference historical eras, famous locations, or specific visual themes to draw from the model's knowledge base.

### 2. Render Precise Text
Enclose desired words in quotes (e.g., `"Happy Birthday"`). Describe the typography style explicitly, such as a `"bold sans-serif font"` or `"neon cursive signage"`.

### 3. Translate and Localize Ideas
Write the prompt in one language and specify the target language and regional/cultural cues. Provide exact translated text in quote marks to guide the rendering.

### 4. Prompt for Production-Ready Specs
Specify the desired aspect ratio (like `4:3`, `1:1`, or `9:16`) and describe the target format (e.g., `"widescreen backdrop"`, `"vertical social post"`).

### 5. Ask for Multiple Images at Once
Ask for variations (e.g. `"three distinct variations of a product mockup"` or `"four different color palettes"`) to compare side-by-side.

### 6. Maintain Subject Consistency
Upload clear reference images and assign a distinct name to each character/object in your prompt so the model can follow along as you build out scenes.
